[normal] Provide a description of the target audience(s) that will be the focus of effort for the duration of the project. 8,000 character limit.
[normal] The key target audience for this project includes cotton farmers throughout the US Cotton Belt, National Cotton Council, Cotton Incorporated, Textile Exchange, US Cotton Trust Protocol, Climate Beneficial Fiber, various cotton merchandisers, Soil Health Institute, University Extension, county extension agents, graduate and undergraduate students, as well as the general public interested in learning sustainable cotton production.
[normal] The key target audiences for this project include organic cotton farmers across Texas and the broader U.S. Cotton Belt, with a particular focus on those adopting or considering regenerative and organic production practices. Producer organizations such as the Texas Organic Cotton Marketing Cooperative (TOCMC) and the Texas Organic Farmers and Gardeners Association (TOFGA) are also central audiences, serving as critical partners for outreach, demonstration, and knowledge exchange.
[normal] County and regional extension agents represent another important audience, as they play a pivotal role in translating research findings into practical recommendations for farmers. Organic cotton ginning mills, merchandisers, and other supply chain partners are engaged to ensure that research outcomes are aligned with market needs and production standards. Carbon market providers and relevant USDA agencies, including the Risk Management Agency (RMA), are included to support the development of sustainable management incentives, economic analyses, and risk mitigation strategies for organic cotton producers.
[normal] The project also targets academic audiences, including undergraduate and graduate students involved in research training, laboratory work, and field experiments. These efforts provide hands-on experiential learning while developing the next generation of agronomists, soil scientists, and sustainable agriculture professionals.
[normal] In addition to these primary audiences, broader stakeholders and the general public interested in sustainable and organic agriculture will benefit from outreach efforts. This includes participants in field days, workshops, online seminars, educational materials, and public communications, all designed to raise awareness of organic cotton production, regenerative practices, and climate-smart agriculture.
[normal] By engaging a diverse set of stakeholders spanning producers, educators, supply chain participants, and policymakers, this project aims to create a comprehensive network of knowledge exchange and practical application. Collectively, these efforts ensure that research outcomes are translated into actionable strategies, the adoption of sustainable practices is facilitated, and awareness of organic cotton production is increased at local, regional, and national levels.
[normal] What are the major goals of this project?
[normal] The long-term goal of the proposed research is to apply improved precision management practices to enable enhanced carbon sequestration and reduced greenhouse gas emission, pest control, and nutrient and water management; and to address labor challenges, steward land, create new market opportunities, and ensure a sustainable supply of climate-smart cotton.
[normal] The specific objectives of this project are:
[normal] 1. Develop regenerative cotton production practices and investigate their long-term effects on US Belt-wide cotton production systems.

[normal] Nithya Rajan (Obj-1.1, 1.2.1, 1.2.2, 1.2.3, 1.2.4, 2.2), Texas A&M University
[normal] During the 2025 reporting period, field experiments were conducted at the Texas A&M Research Farm in Snook, Burleson County, Texas, to evaluate the effects of bed configuration and cover crop termination timing on cotton productivity. Treatments included non-raised bed, raised bed, and raised furrow planting systems, with and without cover crops terminated two and four weeks before planting, arranged in a randomized complete block design. Research activities included cover crop termination, cotton planting, field management, biomass sampling, and manual harvest to determine lint yield. Yield data were collected, and preliminary analyses were completed to compare treatment effects among management systems. Results showed that non-raised bed treatments produced slightly higher lint yields than raised bed systems, although treatment differences were not statistically significant. Cover crop treatments generally maintained or improved yield performance, particularly under the two-week termination treatment. These activities contributed to evaluating regenerative cotton management strategies for improving long-term sustainability in Texas Blackland Vertisols.

[normal] Objective 3. Evaluate the economic feasibility of precision regenerative production practices and determine new market opportunities.
[normal] Darren Hudson (Obj-3), Texas Tech University
[normal] We completed a meta-analysis of precision application and soil health agronomic practices. Data on yield, profitability, and environmental outcomes were collected from U.S.-based agronomic research involving guidance systems, variable rate application, cover crops, and pasture management across US crops (primarily corn, wheat, and cotton), along with fruits and vegetables and pastures. Findings indicates for cotton that guidance systems and variable rate planters were the most important for yield and profit improvements. In addition to this, we completed an analysis of the risk and insurance impacts of soil health improvements in cotton. Analysis was based on a Monte Carlo simulation using agronomic data from the Texas High Plains and simulated yield impacts of soil health improvements. Findings indicated that soil health improvements significantly improve yield and reduce the risk of crop failure in dryland cotton systems. However, the increased yield increases insurance costs. However, the added yield benefits outweigh increased insurance costs.

[normal] During the 2025-2026 reporting period, our team completed the following activities toward Objective 4: In April 2025, we facilitated three in-person focus groups in South Georgia with 9 Georgia current or perspective cotton farmers in collaboration with the Soil Health Institute (SHI) to examine the complexity of adoption of regenerative practices, including cover crops, and the challenges cotton producers face in implementing or sustaining these practices. The survey is being conducted with producers participating in SAS CAP soil sampling in Georgia and includes management and demographic questions to increase understanding of the adoption of regenerative practices as a multi-dimensional process and the characteristics of producers who participate in on-farm research. After piloting this survey in GA, we will share it with other SAS CAP teams for implementation in other areas of the US Cotton Belt. In addition, three participatory workshops will be carried out in GA in March 2026 to develop knowledge about GA farmers’ understanding of soil health and soil health management practices.
